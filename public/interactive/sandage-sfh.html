What does τ mean?

A clock for the smooth peak

ψ(t) ∝ (t / τ²) exp[−t² / (2τ²)]

The smooth rate peaks at t = τ after onset. Small τ makes a short, early episode; large τ spreads formation over more time. τ is not an exponential e-folding time.

The equations & normalization

Let t be time since star formation began, from 0 to T = Tform. The continuous component has one unit of formed mass over this interval:

c(t) = (t / τ²) exp[−t² / (2τ²)] / {1 − exp[−T² / (2τ²)]}

For burst i, ai is its lookback age, μi = T − ai is its centre since onset, and Ai = ABURSTi:

gi(t) = exp[−(t − μi)² / (2σi²)] / ∫₀ᵀ exp[−(u − μi)² / (2σi²)] du
ψfraction(t) = [c(t) + Σ Ai gi(t)] / (1 + Σ Ai)

The rate integrated from onset to observation is exactly one in the normalized view. In physical units, ψ = ψfraction Mformed / 10⁹ gives M☉ yr⁻¹ when time is in Gyr. Adding a burst at fixed total mass reallocates mass from the smooth component. The burst’s share of the final formed mass is Ai / (1 + ΣAj), not Ai itself.

The lower panel is F(t) = ∫₀ᵗ ψfraction(u) du. Milestones are solved from this cumulative function. All Gaussian tails outside [0,T] are removed and the remaining profile is renormalized; no stars form before onset or after observation.

A redshift axis changes the horizontal coordinate only: the vertical quantity remains a rate per unit time. Its area in redshift coordinates is not a mass. Comparison curves are each normalized over their own lifetime and use their own saved mass in physical mode.

Sources, assumptions & saving your work

The continuous equation follows the Sandage form shown in Zibetti’s 2017 stellar-population presentation. This is the t exp(−t²/2τ²) form, distinct from the delayed-exponential t exp(−t/τ) form.

Local provenance: PhD2/sci_plots.ipynb, the “SFH Reconstruction” section and reconstruct_sfh function; parameter layout from PhD2/Sandage_z2_lib_v4.4/sandage_varZ_v4.4z2eq.fits, extension 1. Inspected 12 November 2026. The original library generator was not available for verification.

Cosmic ages use Astropy Planck18, including its massive-neutrino settings. The embedded table has 2,001 samples uniformly spaced in log(1+z), from z = 0 to 30. Interpolation is linear in log(age); inverse conversion uses the same table. The table was generated with Astropy 7.2.0.

Save setup downloads parameters and the pinned comparison as JSON. Load setup restores that file. Data CSV exports time coordinates, component rates, and cumulative mass on an adaptive grid resolving narrow bursts. Figure SVG downloads the two-panel vector chart with model metadata. This panel also remembers the latest settings in this browser when local storage is available; Reset returns to the initial example and clears the pinned comparison. No network requests are needed.

Interactive limits are chosen for exploration, not as BaStA priors: 0 ≤ zobs ≤ 15, zobs + 0.01 ≤ zform ≤ 30, 0.01 ≤ τ ≤ 10 Gyr, 0–6 bursts, 0 ≤ ABURST ≤ 3, and 1 ≤ σ ≤ 1,000 Myr.
